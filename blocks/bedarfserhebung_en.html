What Romanian municipalities actually need

We asked 3,001 municipalities how they currently manage stray and owned dogs and where they reach their limits. 105 responses could be analysed. Here are the results – in full and without varnish.

  • Municipalities contacted3,001 
  • Responses received132a response rate of 4.4 %
  • Responses analysed105with explicit consent

How to read these figures

  • Of 3,001 municipalities contacted, 132 responded. The analysis covers the 105 responses for which explicit consent to processing was given.
  • A response rate of 4.4 % means the participants are a self-selected group – presumably municipalities with above-average administrative capacity, a perceived pressure to act, or both.
  • The results should be read as a picture of the responding municipalities, not as a representative cross-section of all Romanian municipalities.
  • Municipalities are reported at county level only, never by name. Data as of 7 September 2026.

Where the shoe pinches most

Seven areas, rated on a scale from 1 to 5

Municipalities rated seven areas: 1 = no problem, 5 = major problem. Shown is the average across all 105 responses. Two areas need an explanation, because their labels can be misread:

Legal / administrative requirements

The bureaucratic effort of implementation – approval procedures, public procurement rules, reporting obligations towards the county council and the prefecture. This does not refer to legal questions of animal welfare itself.

Public acceptance / complaints

The extent to which implementation is hampered by a lack of acceptance among residents – complaints about stray dogs, but also resistance to particular measures.

  • Legal / administrative req.Ø 2.83
  • Acceptance / complaintsØ 2.71
  • Veterinary capacityØ 2.70
  • Funding / budgetØ 2.45
  • Adoption / rehomingØ 2.27

Worth noting

Neither money nor staff come first, but bureaucracy and local acceptance.

All seven values are below 3 – on average the burden is rated as moderate. What matters is therefore less the absolute level than the ranking.

Municipalities and organisations are not finding each other

What we take from the survey

The most common reason why a municipality has never worked with an animal welfare organisation is neither a lack of need nor rejection – it is a lack of knowledge. At the same time, numerous organisations and mobile neutering teams work in Romania that could close precisely this gap.

That is why our next step is to build a directory sorted by region: organisations and neutering teams active in Romania – so that every municipality can see who works nearby.
